01 / PROBLEM

Plan for action, not more cognitive load.

Planning can become another source of friction when tasks, chapters, deadlines, and revision compete for attention. The interface should reveal the next useful action without requiring a complex system to be learned first.

02 / RESEARCH + USERS

Turn assumptions into questions.

The research plan focuses on how students currently plan study work, where rescheduling breaks down, and what information is actually needed in a daily view.

Planning interviews
Explore goals, routines, terminology, and planning habits.
Task observation
Observe creating, completing, and rescheduling a study task.
Friction mapping
Capture overload, missed context, and unclear status.
Constraint review
Consider small screens, intermittent connectivity, and accessibility.
03 / JOURNEY + FLOW

Keep the journey predictable.

Move from a high-level plan to subject/chapter context and then to today's next action. Completion and rescheduling should be understandable at the point of action.

Open planner
Choose day
Open task
Study / complete
Reschedule / return
04 / WIREFRAME + UI

Show status without visual noise.

Progress, planned work, completed work, and the current day use clear hierarchy and compact states rather than relying on dense dashboards.

05 / TEST + IMPROVE

Validate planning and rescheduling.

Testing should focus on whether people can understand the current day, complete a task, and change a plan without losing context.

Usability
Test create, complete, and reschedule tasks.
Iteration
Record changes with the reason and unresolved questions.
Accessibility
Check keyboard access, focus, labels, contrast, and touch targets.
Privacy
Keep study data collection minimal and transparent.
VALIDATION STATUS
No fabricated test results are presented. Findings will be added only after actual testing.
06 / OUTCOME

Define success before claiming it.

Success would mean the planner helps students identify what to do next, understand progress, and adjust plans without unnecessary complexity. The next evidence milestone is real task-based usability testing.
